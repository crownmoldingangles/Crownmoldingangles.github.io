What Is a Spring Angle?

The spring angle is the angle at which crown molding (or crown moulding, as it's spelled in Canada and the UK) sits against the wall. When crown molding is installed, it doesn't sit flat — it bridges diagonally across the wall-ceiling junction. The spring angle describes how steep that diagonal is.

The spring angle is critical for calculating miter and bevel cuts. Using the wrong spring angle in your calculations will produce cuts that don't fit, no matter how carefully you measure the corner. Always verify your spring angle before calculating.

The Three Common Spring Angles

38° Spring Angle

Most Common

Also written as 52/38 (52° wall angle / 38° ceiling angle)

  • Most widely available at hardware stores
  • Sits flatter against the ceiling
  • Smaller profile projection from wall
  • Best choice for DIY projects
  • Standard 90° miter: 31.62° / 33.85°

45° Spring Angle

Standard

Also written as 45/45 (equal wall and ceiling angles)

  • Common in decorative and larger profiles
  • Projects further from wall and ceiling
  • More dramatic, traditional appearance
  • Simpler nested cutting math
  • Standard 90° miter: 35.26° / 30.00°

52° Spring Angle

Less Common

Also written as 38/52 (38° wall angle / 52° ceiling angle)

  • Less common, typically custom or high-end
  • Sits closer to the wall than ceiling
  • Larger profile with dramatic projection
  • Requires precise calculation
  • Standard 90° miter: 38.94° / 25.24°

How to Identify Your Spring Angle

There are three ways to find your spring angle:

  1. Check the packaging. Most molding packaging lists the spring angle directly, often as a fraction like 52/38 or 45/45.
  2. Measure it yourself. Place the crown molding flat on a table, back side up. Use a digital angle finder to measure the angle between the back flat of the molding and the table surface. This is the spring angle.
  3. Use the 3-4-5 method. Hold the molding in its installed position in a corner (no glue). Measure 3 inches along the wall and 4 inches along the ceiling from the corner. If the molding touches both marks, it's approximately 45°. If it touches the wall mark but not the ceiling, it leans more toward 38°.
💡 Quick Identification Tip If you bought your molding at Home Depot or Lowe's and don't have the packaging, it's almost certainly 38° spring angle — it accounts for the vast majority of residential crown molding sold at big-box stores.

How Spring Angle Affects Cut Angles

The spring angle is one of the two key inputs for calculating crown molding cuts (the other is the corner angle). Here's how the standard 90° corner miter and bevel settings differ across spring angles:

Spring Angle Miter (Laying Flat) Bevel (Laying Flat) Miter (Nested) Bevel (Nested)
38°31.62°33.85°45.00°0°
45°35.26°30.00°45.00°0°
52°38.94°25.24°45.00°0°

Note that the nested method always gives 45° miter with 0° bevel for a 90° corner — regardless of spring angle. This is one reason many DIYers prefer the nested method: you don't need to worry about spring angle when using a crown stop jig.

⚠️ Don't Mix Spring Angles in One Room If you're adding crown to a room that already has crown molding, you must match the existing spring angle exactly. Even a slight difference will be noticeable where new and old molding meets. Bring a sample of existing molding to the hardware store to match the profile before buying new material.

Which Spring Angle Should You Choose?

  • 38° for most DIY projects — easiest to find, lowest cost, works with all saw types
  • 45° for a more traditional or dramatic look — good for larger rooms and higher ceilings
  • 52° for matching existing molding — only if you already have 52° in the space
  • Custom angle — use the calculator's custom input for any non-standard spring angle

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I measure the spring angle of existing crown molding?

Hold a piece of the crown molding in its installed position (or against a scrap of wall-ceiling angle). Place a digital angle finder against the flat back of the molding and the wall surface. The reading is the spring angle. Alternatively, remove a piece and measure it flat on a table as described above.

Can I use 38° settings on 45° spring angle molding?

No — using the wrong spring angle produces cuts that won't fit. The miter and bevel differ by several degrees between spring angles, causing visible gaps. Always measure or confirm your spring angle before cutting.

What does 52/38 mean on crown molding?

The two numbers represent the angles the molding makes with the wall and ceiling. 52/38 means the molding makes a 52° angle with the wall and a 38° angle with the ceiling — making the spring angle 38°. The two numbers always add up to 90°.

Does a larger spring angle mean a bigger looking crown?

Not directly — the visual size of crown molding depends more on its face width than the spring angle. However, a larger spring angle does make the molding project further from the wall and ceiling, which can make it appear more substantial from below.
