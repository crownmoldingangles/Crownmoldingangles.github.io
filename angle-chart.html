How to Use This Chart

✅ Step-by-Step Instructions:
  1. Identify your spring angle: Check the packaging or measure with a protractor
  2. Measure your wall corner: Use a digital angle finder for accuracy
  3. Find your angles: Look up the miter and bevel in the chart above
  4. Set your saw: Adjust both miter and bevel to match
  5. Test cut: Always make a test cut on scrap wood first!
⚠️ Important Notes:
  • These angles assume "laying flat" method: Crown molding placed upside down on the saw table
  • Walls are rarely perfectly 90°: Always measure your actual corner angle
  • For vaulted ceilings: These charts don't apply - use our calculator instead
  • Left vs Right: Refers to which side of the miter saw you're cutting on

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know my crown molding spring angle?

Check the packaging - it's usually labeled as 52/38 (38° spring), 45/45 (45° spring), or 38/52 (52° spring). If unmarked, place a piece in a framing square and measure the angle it makes with the wall.

Why are my cuts still not fitting perfectly?

Most likely your walls aren't exactly 90 degrees. Measure your actual corner angle with a digital angle finder and use our calculator to get precise angles for your specific corner.

What does "laying flat" mean?

The "upside down and backwards" method where you lay the crown molding flat on your saw table with the ceiling edge down. The angles in this chart assume this cutting method.

Can I use these angles for vaulted ceilings?

No - vaulted and cathedral ceilings require different calculations because the ceiling slope changes the spring angle. Use our vaulted ceiling guide instead.

Do inside and outside corners use different angles?

The miter and bevel angles are the same, but you position the molding differently on the saw. For inside corners, place the bottom edge against the fence. For outside corners, place the top edge against the fence.
